## Overview

v2.5.0 publishes the SD1.5 quantization theory — not the recipe, the argument behind it. The claim is
narrow and testable: for a deep UNet, the layer that matters is the layer whose quantization moves the
**sampler's output**, and that is not the layer whose **weight** distorts most. Everything below follows
from taking that sentence seriously: how the criterion is measured, why it must be measured on a
trajectory and in the original frame, what the measured curve says about how much protection is worth
paying for, and where the argument stops.

## 1. Why the previous selector failed — stated as a measurement, not an opinion

The previous selector chose its FP16 protection set from **weight-space** statistics: kurtosis, outlier
ratio, absmax, and a weighted-histogram **MSE** of the quantized weight at the absmax pack point, screened
through a DualMonitor activation profile
([the retired selector](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/archives/quantize_sd1.5_hswq_v1.0.py),
[why the old selector failed](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/How%20to%20quantize%20SD1.5.md#why-the-old-selector-failed)).

That family of statistics answers *how much does this layer's weight distort*. It does not answer *how much
does this layer move the output*. An SD1.5 UNet is a deep series of blocks: a layer's weight error is
amplified, attenuated, or **cancelled** by everything downstream, and a weight-space MSE cannot see that
propagation.

The measured consequence, on the checkpoint used throughout: the V1.0 protection set scored **the same as
native** — no protection at all. The protection bought nothing, and the defect was the **criterion**, not
the quantizer. That is the finding this release builds on: a protection budget spent by the wrong ranking
is not a smaller gain, it is no gain.

## 2. The criterion: trajectory impact

For each ConvRot-eligible layer, one at a time
([what is measured](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/How%20to%20quantize%20SD1.5.md#what-is-measured)):

1. hold every other layer at its pristine weight;
2. replace **this one layer's weight** by its ConvRot INT8 reconstruction **in the original frame**
   — `dequant(INT8(W @ H^T))` = `(q*s) @ H`;
3. run the **same fixed denoising trajectory** (25 steps, fixed seed, fixed latent, fixed context);
4. record the divergence from the pristine trajectory, `rel_mse(x_perturbed, x_pristine)`.

The resulting number is the layer's **trajectory impact**: how far the sampler's output moves when only
that layer is quantized. It is a property of the sampler plus the layer, not of the weight alone.

---

## 3. Why a trajectory, and not a single forward

A single forward measures a layer's sensitivity at the one timestep the sampler happens to call it. A
trajectory measures what the sampler experiences: the error enters at one step, is carried in the latent,
and is amplified or cancelled by every later step
([why the trajectory](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/How%20to%20quantize%20SD1.5.md#why-the-trajectory-and-not-a-single-forward)).

The difference is not cosmetic. On the measured checkpoint the two criteria **disagree on 38 of the top 80
layers** (42/80 overlap):

| layer | trajectory rank | single-forward rank |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| `output_blocks.6.0.skip_connection` | **1** | 5 |
| `output_blocks.9.0.skip_connection` | **2** | 13 |
| `out.2` | **3** | 22 |
| `output_blocks.11.1.proj_in` | **4** | 38 |
| `input_blocks.1.1.proj_in` | 58 | **1** |
| `input_blocks.2.1.proj_in` | 55 | **2** |
| `input_blocks.2.1.attn1.to_k` | 223 | 6 |

The trajectory ranks the **decoder-side** `skip_connection` lines and output layers highest, because their
error reaches the final latent directly and is not diluted by later blocks. A single forward at one
timestep ranks the **encoder-side** early `proj_in` transitions highest, because they move that one step the
most while part of their error is absorbed downstream.

---

## 4. Why the original frame

The pack stores the **rotated** weight and the kernel rotates the **activation**, so the error that reaches
the output is `(q*s) @ H - W`. Injecting in the rotated frame (`q*s`) measures the rotation itself rather
than the error, and **inverts the ranking**
([why the original frame](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/How%20to%20quantize%20SD1.5.md#why-the-original-frame)).

This is the same rule the SDXL track arrived at independently, and it is the reason a ranking measured in
the wrong frame can look reasonable and be wrong.

## 5. Method 1 — the CLI pipeline

```
<base>  (FP16 checkpoint, 2033.8 MB)
  |  Step 1: measure the trajectory impact
  |          sd1.5/diagimpact_sd15.py
  v
impact_sd15_traj_<model>.json   (282 layers)
  |  Step 2: build the pack
  |          sd1.5/quantize_sd1.5_hswq_v1.1.py --top-k <K>
  v
<pack>  (K protected in FP16, the rest ConvRot INT8; CLIP untouched; VAE dropped)
  |  Step 3: the acceptance
  |          benchmark/sd1.5_int8_traj_compare.py (25 seeds)
  v
PASS / FAIL
```

### 5.1 Step 1 — the trajectory impact

[`sd1.5/diagimpact_sd15.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/sd1.5/diagimpact_sd15.py)
runs the measurement across all 282 ConvRot-eligible UNet layers
([Step 1](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/How%20to%20quantize%20SD1.5.md#step-1---the-trajectory-impact)).

- **25 steps by default** — SD1.5's production step count, the same one the acceptance gate and the
  calibration use. 12 is the Z-Image/NVFP4 default and is too short here, so the count is fixed rather than
  inherited.
- Writes `impact/impact_sd15_traj_<model stem>.json` when `--out` is omitted.
- Measured cost on one consumer GPU: **one 25-step trajectory is 0.98 s**, so 282 layers × 2 seeds is about
  **12 minutes** — not hours. This matters for the method's usability: a criterion that takes hours per
  checkpoint would push users back to weight statistics.
- Progress prints every 10 layers. **The ranking does not transfer between checkpoints.**

### 5.2 Step 2 — build the pack

[`sd1.5/quantize_sd1.5_hswq_v1.1.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/sd1.5/quantize_sd1.5_hswq_v1.1.py)
consumes the impact JSON and writes the hybrid pack
([Step 2](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/How%20to%20quantize%20SD1.5.md#step-2---build-the-pack)).

| Component | Treatment |
| :--- | :--- |
| quantized | every 2D `Linear` **and** 4D `Conv2d` under `model.diffusion_model.`, except the top-K |
| protected | the top-K layers, plus every 1D tensor — **all stored FP16** |
| CLIP | **copied through unchanged** (`cond_stage_model.`): same keys, same dtypes, zero `.comfy_quant` / `.weight_scale` artifacts |
| VAE | **dropped** — `first_stage_model.` is not written; decode with a standalone SD1.5 VAE |
| scale | INT8 `weight_scale` is FP32 by design; its rank matches the weight (`(O,1)` for 2D, `(O,1,1,1)` for 4D) |

**Bias correction is opt-in** (`--bias_correction`, default OFF). When enabled it folds the systematic INT8
output shift into the layer bias:

```
E[(W_q - W) x]  ~  (W_q - W) @ E[x]
bias_new = bias_orig - delta
```

with `E[x]` the per-input-channel activation mean collected **in the same run** from the V1.0 calibration
(32 prompts × 25 steps, seed 42, via `StableDiffusionPipeline`). It applies to **INT8 layers only** — a
protected layer is untouched, weights and bias alike.

The builder reports the honest count —
`the bias-corrected layers: N (skipped: A had no act_mean, B had a shape mismatch)` — and it **aborts**
rather than saving a pack labelled bias-corrected when N is 0. A label the file cannot back is a defect, so
the tool refuses to produce it.

### 5.3 Step 3 — the acceptance

[`benchmark/sd1.5_int8_traj_compare.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/benchmark/sd1.5_int8_traj_compare.py)
runs the deterministic 25-seed comparison (25 steps, 512×512, cfg 7.0) against the FP16 base
([Step 3](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/How%20to%20quantize%20SD1.5.md#step-3---the-acceptance)).

- **0/25 bifurcated** is a hard condition. `drifted` (gradual divergence) is acceptable; `bifurcated` (a
  sudden trajectory jump) is not — it means a different picture, not a softer one.
- The mean target is written as **≥ 0.97** in the CLI section and as **≥ 0.95** in the in-workflow node
  section, where the k=80 sweep is quoted at **0.97622**. Both figures are in the document as written; the
  adopted k=80 pack clears either.
- The seed list is fixed, so the comparison is between packs on the same trajectories rather than a fresh
  sample each run.

## 8. The measured curve

Measured on `0.5xxmix9realistic_v40 0.5BRA7` — 25 seeds × 25 steps, 512×512, cfg 7.0
([measured results](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/How%20to%20quantize%20SD1.5.md#measured-results-on-05xxmix9realistic_v40-05bra7)):

| pack | protection criterion | protected | mean | same-image | bifurcated | size |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| native | none | 0 | 0.96265 | 13/25 | 0/25 | 1058.6 MB |
| k40 | trajectory | 40 | 0.96212 | 14/25 | 0/25 | ~1115 MB |
| **k80** | single forward | **80** | **0.97622** | **18/25** | **0/25** | 1167.5 MB |
| k80 | trajectory | 80 | 0.97542 | 16/25 | 0/25 | 1160.4 MB |
| k100 | single forward | 100 | 0.974 | — | 0/25 | 1193.5 MB |

### 8.1 What the curve says

**The protection works, and only above a threshold.** Native sits at 0.96265 and k40 at 0.96212 —
identical. Protecting 40 layers buys nothing; the gain appears between k40 and k80 (+0.0133), so the layers
that matter are concentrated in that band, not in the top 40.

**More protection is not better.** k100 (0.974) is *below* k80 (0.97622) and 26 MB larger. Two effects push
against each other:

- a protected layer keeps its FP16 weights, so it carries no quantization error — but it also **receives no
  bias correction**;
- an INT8 layer absorbs bias correction, which cancels its systematic error.

Past k80, promoting more layers to FP16 loses more bias correction than it gains in weight precision. The
optimum is therefore a property of the *pack*, not of the ranking.

**The two criteria tie at k=80.** Single-forward 0.97622 against trajectory 0.97542 — a difference of
0.0008 against a per-seed spread of 0.80 to 0.999. They agree on the outcome while disagreeing on 38 of the
top 80 layers, which is exactly why single-layer contributions cannot be added up.

**The size ceiling is part of the theory.** The source is 2033.8 MB and the practical target is about
1.16 GB, so the packs above sit at that limit. SD1.5 has no room for a combined multi-metric selection the
way SDXL does; it must pick one criterion and spend the protection budget well.

## 9. Honest limits

Written into the document rather than left for a reader to discover
([honest limits](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/How%20to%20quantize%20SD1.5.md#honest-limits)):

- **No criterion here is globally optimal.** Every method — static statistics, single forward, trajectory —
  measures **one layer at a time** and ranks. What actually happens is tens of layers quantized
  simultaneously, where errors interact, and that cannot be recovered from single-layer measurements.
- **Single-layer contributions are not additive.** Two criteria that disagree on 38 of 80 layers can
  produce the same final score; two that agree can still differ. The score at a given K has to be measured,
  not predicted.
- **The ranking is checkpoint-specific.** Impact order, K and the trajectory numbers must be re-measured
  for every model. Nothing here transfers, which is also why the tool prints progress rather than caching a
  ranking across models.
- **The acceptance uses fixed seeds and prompts**, so it is a relative comparison between packs, not an
  absolute quality claim.

Read the table as *"among the candidates measured under this criterion, this one is best"* — not as *"this
is the optimum"*.

## 10. The loader trap

An INT8 Conv2d pack **must** be loaded through the HSWQ loader
([the loader trap](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/How%20to%20quantize%20SD1.5.md#the-loader-trap-read-before-debugging-nan)).
Three facts, and getting any of them wrong yields NaN with `int8_linear` calls at 0:

1. `comfy_kitchen` has **no INT8 conv kernel** — only `int8_linear`. The conv path exists solely in the
   loader's `_patch_ops_decode_and_conv()`.
2. That patch is gated on a thread-local: `_should_inject_int8_conv()` returns
   `bool(_int8_quant_conv_tls.active)`. Calling `apply_comfy_quant_int8_patches()` alone does not open it.
3. There are **two vendored copies** of `comfy_quant_int8` — one under `patches/`, one under
   `nodes/sd1.5 int8/` — each with its own thread-local. The SD1.5 loader collects every
   `_int8_quant_conv_tls` in `sys.modules` and opens them all.

Use [`sd1.5/verify_sd15_loader.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/sd1.5/verify_sd15_loader.py),
which drives `load_checkpoint_sd15_hswq_weight_dtype`, so the check goes through the path production uses
rather than a direct graph that bypasses the patch.

## 12. What this release does not claim

- It does not claim a better quantizer. The INT8 round trip is unchanged from v2.4.7; what changed is the
  ranking that decides which layers keep FP16.
- It does not claim the k=80 point transfers. K is a checkpoint property and is re-measured per model.
- It does not claim the trajectory criterion beats single forward on this checkpoint — they tie at k=80.
  The release states the disagreement in ranking, the tie in outcome, and the reason a single-layer
  measurement cannot settle it.

**Full diff:** [`v2.4.9...v2.5.0`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/compare/v2.4.9...v2.5.0)
