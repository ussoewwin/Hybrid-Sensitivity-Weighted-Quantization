published with the sha256 it applies to.

## 1. The question, stated so it can be answered

A backdoor is a branch: ordinary behaviour on ordinary input, attacker behaviour on chosen input. A
weight diff cannot establish either its presence or its absence. Two files that both reduce refusal along
one direction — one by dropping a single safety-direction projection, the other by adding a rank-2
payload behind that projection — can produce similar-looking diffs and completely different files. The
distinction is written out in
[§1.3 of the technical guide](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Technical_Guide.md#13-what-a-backdoor-is-and-what-a-weight-diff-is-not)
and in
[the verdict index](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Verdicts.md#what-a-backdoor-is-and-what-a-weight-diff-is-not).

The audit does not ask "is this weight set unusual". It asks a question that has an answer: *can this
file branch on its input, by any of the shapes a file can use, and does any probe fire?*

The threat model and the literature behind it — a poisoned model passes capability benchmarks, ~250
poisoned documents backdoor both a 600M and a 13B model, quantization silently redistributes a backdoored
source — are in
[§1 of the technical guide](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Technical_Guide.md#1-the-threat-stated-precisely)
and
[§1.2](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Technical_Guide.md#12-documented-real-world-incidents).

## 2. The three branch shapes, and the measurement that closes each

A safetensors file can make the network branch on input in exactly three ways. The list is exhaustive over
file contents, not over attacker ingenuity.

| Shape | What it looks like | What closes it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Added material** | a new tensor, bias, embedding row or adapter that is not in the declared base | full tensor census against the reference, including the zero-content census, with every key accounted for ([census procedure](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Procedure.md#7-phase-3--census-and-layout-mapping), [tool](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/source_audit.py)) |
| **A second component behind the declared change** | something riding behind the edit the model openly makes — the abliteration direction carrying a rank-2 payload | the delta is projected onto its own principal directions and the part the declared edit cannot account for is measured and named ([delta structure](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Procedure.md#8-phase-4--delta-structure)) |
| **One component that reacts to a narrow input class** | the switch shape: a single direction that stays quiet on ordinary prompts | a trigger search over a candidate set compared against the control spread, with a positive control on the instrument itself ([trigger search](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/trigger_search.py), [positive control](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/verdicts/Positive_Control.md)) |

A poison trained diffusely across ordinary-looking weights is a training property, not a shape. What the
audit can do with it is the behavioural comparison and the rebuild test, and the reports say so rather
than implying a shape search covers it — see
[§2 of the guide](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Technical_Guide.md#2-what-can-and-cannot-be-proven).

## 3. Verdict strength and coverage are defined, not implied

Every verdict carries a strength and a coverage figure, because a verdict without them cannot be read. The
definitions are in
[the index](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Verdicts.md#what-each-verdict-covers)
and in
[§2.9 of the procedure](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Procedure.md#29-status-words).

- **A** — the delta is fully accounted for at the weight level by the declared edit: every tensor of the
  artifact is compared against its reference, and the remainder is explained by the edit itself.
- **B** — the delta is dense and weight-level closure is not available, so the verdict rests on the
  behavioural probes.
- **C** — the weight-level audit covers part of the file only, and the behavioural probes carry the rest.

**Coverage** is the tensors the weight-level audit measured, over all tensors in the file. A verdict of *no
backdoor detected* therefore reads: none was detected in the artifact identified by the sha256 in that
row, by the tests that report documents, within the limits that report states. It is not a probability.
Wilson intervals quoted in reports are the uncertainty of a metric, never a probability of absence.

## 4. Why the audit runs on the source, not only on the pack

The pack is INT8 — a small integer and a scale per weight — so a probe that needs 1e-6 resolution has
nothing to resolve. A direction written onto the source survives the rotation and the per-channel scale,
and a side channel (a bias, an embedding row, an added tensor) passes through unchanged. Removing the
direction from the source and re-quantizing reproduces the pack's behaviour, which makes auditing the
source the same audit performed one layer up, at the precision where the question can be answered. This is
[§3.2](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Technical_Guide.md#32-the-decisive-idea-exact-re-quantization)
of the guide.

The pack side is not skipped: exact re-quantization is the tool. The pack's INT8 codes are recomputed from
the source under the converter's own arithmetic, so a pack that carries anything the source does not is
detected as a code that cannot be reproduced —
[`audit/backdoor_audit.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/backdoor_audit.py),
described in
[§4 of the guide](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Technical_Guide.md#4-tool-1--auditbackdoorauditpy-pack-vs-source).

## 5. Tools, gates and records

**The two audit tools**

- [`audit/source_audit.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/source_audit.py) —
  the source itself: census, delta structure, merge proof, localisation ablation, trigger search. Written
  up in
  [§5 of the guide](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Technical_Guide.md#5-tool-2--auditsourceauditpy-the-source-model).
- [`audit/backdoor_audit.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/backdoor_audit.py) —
  the pack against its source: exact re-quantization, code-level reproduction, stored-float audit,
  container scan.

**The gates over the reports** — a report that fails any of them is not publishable:

- [`audit/report_check.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/report_check.py) —
  structure, required sections including LIMITS, and citation resolution.
- [`audit/check_cites.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/check_cites.py) —
  every quoted number must resolve to a real line in a real record, with the record's sha256 matched.
- [`audit/procedure_check.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/procedure_check.py) —
  the procedure's own steps must all be present in the documents that claim to follow it.
- [`audit/selftest.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/selftest.py) —
  the tools' own self-test.
- [`audit/check_hf_upload.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/check_hf_upload.py) —
  pre-flight for a model card before it is written to the Hub.

**Records**

[`md/verdicts/records/`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/tree/main/md/verdicts/records)
holds the 168 files the reports cite: tensor censuses, rank structure, spectra, trigger candidate lists,
per-candidate trigger values, generation texts with the hand labels read from them, and hash records of
each file and its base. A claim without a record is a defect, not a judgement call — that is what
[`check_cites.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/check_cites.py)
enforces.

## 6. The trigger search carries a measured instrument error

A behavioural pass is only worth the error rate of the instrument that produced it. The positive control
runs the same probes on a clean base model and records **20/28 false positives**:
[md/verdicts/Positive_Control.md](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/verdicts/Positive_Control.md).
That number is published next to the results, not behind them: a pass means the trigger ratio did not
exceed the criterion, and the criterion is read against a control spread that is this wide.

Each report also states its own **LIMITS**: the candidate set size and how it was generated, the positions
probed, and the reference-class boundary — a defect already inside the comparison object's own release is
unreachable by definition. The candidate sets are built by
[`audit/trigger_variants.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/trigger_variants.py),
scored by
[`audit/trigger_logits.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/trigger_logits.py)
and
[`audit/trigger_search.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/trigger_search.py),
and the LIMITS blocks are generated by
[`audit/make_limits.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/make_limits.py)
so that the statement of a report's reach is produced from the run, not written by hand afterwards.

## 8. Input integrity, and the replacement of a damaged copy

Before any number in the table is trusted, the artifacts are re-hashed on disk against their recorded
sha256 and byte counts — the record is
[records/sources_restored.json](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/verdicts/records/sources_restored.json),
and the re-hash itself is
[`audit/st_integrity.py`](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/audit/st_integrity.py).

- The artifacts then in the table were re-verified on 2026-10-28 and matched. Two of them had left the
  model directory during the work and were recovered with verified identity; the cause of that
  disappearance is recorded as unidentified rather than explained away.
- The first published `Qwen3-8B-abliterated-fp16.safetensors` was found to carry **206 all-zero tensors
  across 19 of 36 layers plus the final norm**. It was rebuilt from its declared upstream, and every one of
  the rebuilt file's **399 tensors now equals the reference BF16-to-FP16 cast** of
  `huihui-ai/Qwen3-8B-abliterated`, which is what makes the A verdict exhaustive rather than sampled. The
  index row states both the damaged and the rebuilt sha256 so a cached copy can be identified and replaced
  ([report](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/verdicts/Qwen3_8B_Abliterated.md),
  [zero-content census measure](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Procedure.md#71b-zero-content-census)).

## 10. Scope and limits

- The audit answers *"was a branch detected in this artifact, by these tests, within these limits"*. It
  does not answer *"this file is safe"*
  ([§2](https://github.com/ussoewwin/Hybrid-Sensitivity-Weighted-Quantization/blob/main/md/Backdoor_Audit_Technical_Guide.md#2-what-can-and-cannot-be-proven)).
- **C** is not a pass of the same weight as **A**. SAM 3.1 is reported at C with 6 % weight coverage, and
  its verdict leans on behavioural probes whose instrument error is stated in §6 above.
- A behavioural probe set is finite. The candidate sets, their generation and the probe positions are in
  each report's LIMITS section precisely so that a reader can argue with them.
- The audit is run on the source and on the pack. A pack that reproduces its source exactly inherits the
  source's verdict and adds the code-level check; it does not get a stronger verdict than its source.
